How to share the mental load at home (without a spreadsheet fight)

5 min read · OneSpace

The mental load is not the chores themselves. It is remembering that they exist: noticing the milk is low, knowing the car needs a service, keeping track of whose birthday is next. When one person carries all of it, resentment grows quietly. Here is a calm way to share it.

Make the invisible visible

  1. For one week, both of you jot down every “I need to remember…” thought.
  2. Put everything in one shared place, not in two heads and five chats.
  3. Look at the list together without blame. Most couples are surprised.

Own outcomes, not tasks

  1. Split by area: “bills are yours, school forms are mine” — including the remembering.
  2. Give every reminder an owner. A reminder for “someone” is a reminder for no one.
  3. Use repeats for anything recurring, so nobody re-remembers the same thing monthly.

Decide once, together

  1. Big purchases and plans go in one place with options side by side.
  2. Each of you marks what you like; talk only about the gap.
  3. Record the decision and link the reminder that follows from it.

Keep a weekly 10-minute reset

  1. Sunday evening: look at the next seven days together.
  2. Move anything stuck, reassign anything unfair.
  3. End with one fun plan — the relationship is not a to-do list.
