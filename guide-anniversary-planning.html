Never forget an anniversary again: a gentle planning system

5 min read · OneSpace

Forgetting an important date rarely means you don’t care — it usually means it lived only in your head. A tiny system fixes that for good, and makes the day itself better, because you have time to plan.

Write down the dates that matter

  1. The obvious ones: birthdays, wedding anniversary, engagement.
  2. The quiet ones: the day you met, your first trip, the day you moved in.
  3. Track both the date itself and its yearly anniversary — “8 years since” feels different from “in 12 days”.

Start earlier than you think

  1. Set a “start preparing” reminder three weeks ahead for big dates, one week for small ones.
  2. Book anything that sells out — restaurants, flights, hotels — first.
  3. Set a budget so the day is joyful, not stressful.

Keep a running gift-idea list

  1. When your partner mentions something they like, save it that minute.
  2. Note price and how sure you are; mark it bought when it is.
  3. Keep the list private — some things are better as surprises.

Turn the day into a memory

  1. Write a letter in advance that opens that morning.
  2. Take one photo of both of you, every year, in the same pose.
  3. Save it to a scrapbook — in ten years you’ll be glad you did.
