Living apart · 12 journeys

Different homes, different time zones, shared decisions and small acts of love. See how the pieces of a day stay connected.

01A course decision, made together from 5,700 miles apartLina wants to change her career. Omar doesn't want distance to keep him out of the decision.
How their day unfolded
01

Lina wakes in London and opens Today. In Since You Were Away she sees Omar's update: he watched the show she recommended during his evening. There's also a hug he sent before going to sleep. She opens it, smiles at his note, and feels included in his night despite the distance.

02

Before work, her repeating morning checklist appears for today's cycle: medication, lunch, badge, keys. Yesterday's ticks haven't carried over. She checks each item, catches the forgotten badge, and arrives at work on time. At lunch she remembers the professional course she has been considering and creates a shared Coordinate activity: “Which program should I take?”

03

When Omar wakes in a different time zone, he finds the activity in his updates. He researches three accredited courses, shares their links from his Android browser into OneSpace, and adds each one as an option in Lina's activity. He records tuition, class times in Lina's zone, prerequisites, advantages, and drawbacks instead of sending a stream of disconnected messages.

04

Meanwhile Lina asks two colleagues for recommendations and adds their suggestions to that same activity. She reads Omar's comparison and comments: “Does the evening class include the certificate?” Hours later Omar sees her question, checks the provider details, and updates the option. Their discussion and revisions remain beside the research, even though they were never online together.

05

At the hour they are both free, they open the comparison together. A more expensive course offers flexible evenings and a recognized credential; they choose it and mark the activity Decided. They attach a registration checklist, assign Lina the application and Omar the funding research, add a deadline and reminder, and save the admissions office as a contact card linked to the activity.

06

Lina sends Omar a personal thank-you gesture: “You make big decisions feel less lonely.” Omar opens it after dinner, thousands of miles away. The real achievement isn't just the course: one activity now holds the research, conversation, decision, tasks, reminders and contact, and Omar knows he was genuinely part of Lina's day.

02The visit that started with a flight alert and ended in a memoryA reunion takes more than booking two tickets.
How their day unfolded
01

On Sunday evening Omar receives confirmation of his vacation dates. Instead of texting Lina a screenshot and hoping she finds it later, he creates a shared trip in Coordinate. He adds the date window and a note: “I'd like us to spend one day doing absolutely nothing.” Lina wakes up to the new plan.

02

Lina researches flights while Omar is asleep. She saves three options with arrival times and baggage costs. One fare looks cheap until the suitcase fee is included. Omar checks her options after waking and adds an alternative with a better landing time. They use the comparison to choose a flight that works for both.

03

Once the booking is made, they add flight details and a packing checklist to their shared trip. They save all the documents they will need inside that same trip: booking confirmations, e-tickets, boarding passes when issued, accommodation details, travel insurance and copies of the required travel documents. Lina handles accommodation; Omar checks the files and transport. Both can find what they need and see what is done without searching old chats. They deliberately save a reunion counter so Today shows the days remaining; the trip doesn't create a counter without their choice.

04

They browse Our Places for cafés they saved months ago. Lina chooses a garden for their first afternoon; Omar adds one restaurant to the bucket list. They turn those places into a light itinerary but keep their empty day untouched. When the flight is delayed, Lina finds the details and contact information in one place instead of searching old chats.

05

After the visit, they choose a few favorite photographs and create a shared memory with the place and a little note about that unplanned afternoon. Back in separate homes, they can still open the completed trip and remember not only where they went, but how much work they quietly shared to be there.

03The hard day when a missed call didn't become an argumentThey can't always answer at once. They can still make each other feel heard.
How their day unfolded
01

Lina has a difficult meeting and calls Omar on her walk home. He's asleep; the time difference makes the missed call inevitable. She almost sends an irritated message, then opens OneSpace. In her private Feelings Journal she writes what happened, sorting out the difference between needing advice and simply needing comfort.

02

She makes a daily check-in with a low mood rating and writes: “Rough day. Can we talk when you're awake?” She doesn't share the private journal entry. She also checks Today to see when Omar's normal waking time overlaps her evening, and sends no further messages demanding an immediate reply.

03

Omar wakes and reads the check-in next to the missed call. Instead of guessing why she's upset, he sends a short personal gesture: “I'm here. You don't have to explain everything now.” He sets himself a private reminder to call after his shift and leaves a gentle comment asking whether she wants listening or practical help.

04

When their schedules finally overlap, they talk. Lina has already collected her thoughts; Omar has enough context to listen without turning the conversation into an interrogation. He doesn't attempt to fix her office politics from thousands of miles away. Together they agree she will address the problem in tomorrow's meeting.

05

Later Lina opens the journal and writes one more line: “Today was hard, but I didn't feel alone.” The check-in remains a shared signal, the journal remains private, and the missed call becomes a small example of how to care for each other without demanding constant availability.

Later, when Noor gets promoted, he already knows why it matters to Lina. OneSpace helped him remember, but the interest was his.

They save a screenshot of their call and a note about dinner as a shared memory.

05

The next day Omar marks the occasion plan complete. His budget and gift research stay private; the shared memory belongs to them both. They could have managed this across emails, sticky notes and chats, but OneSpace kept the personal preparation separate from the moment they chose to share.

07Finding a home before they share an addressOne partner can visit apartments. Both deserve a voice in the final choice.
How their day unfolded
01

Lina has accepted a new role in Omar's city, but she will not move for another two months. Omar can attend apartment viewings; Lina can only judge from photos and phone calls. They open a shared Coordinate activity called “Our first place together” and agree on three non-negotiables: quiet nights, train access, and room for two desks.

02

At the first viewing Omar takes photographs of every room and the morning light, checks mobile reception, and saves the agent's contact details. He attaches the photos directly to that apartment's option inside their shared activity and adds its exact location on the map, so Lina can explore the neighborhood from afar. He includes the listing link, monthly rent, deposit, commute and an honest note: “Lovely kitchen, but the street is noisy.” Lina wakes to one complete option—photos, location and all—instead of twelve disconnected pictures.

03

Lina finds two more listings during her lunch break and shares their links into the activity. She asks whether one bedroom has enough room for a desk. Omar adds measurements on his next visit and updates the pros and cons. Over several days, the comments form a clear history of their questions and answers.

04

On Sunday they open the comparison table together. Apartment B is smaller but quieter and closer to both workplaces. They mark it as the decision, link a moving checklist, add application documents and a deadline, and save the agent as a contact card. Omar handles viewing paperwork; Lina handles travel and utility research.

05

When Lina finally opens the apartment door, she recognizes the kitchen and the patch of morning light from Omar's photos. It already feels a little like their home because she helped choose it. Their activity stays available with the decisions, contacts and tasks that made the move possible.

08Being present at a family celebration from another countryOmar cannot attend the party, but he can help Lina make it meaningful.
How their day unfolded
01

Lina's mother is retiring after thirty years as a teacher. The family is planning a dinner, and Omar cannot travel to attend. On a late call Lina admits she feels overwhelmed handling invitations, a cake and a gift while working full-time. Omar suggests they make the celebration a shared OneSpace occasion, even though he will join by video.

02

They create an occasion with the date and a modest spending budget. Lina adds her family's ideas for the venue; Omar compares bakery options online, including pickup times and delivery fees. He saves the bakery's phone number and contact person, while Lina records which relatives have confirmed attendance.

03

A shared checklist divides the preparation: decorations and guest table for Lina, photos and a short speech for Omar. Lina also creates a shared scrapbook in Connect → Memories called “Thirty Years of Teaching.” She chooses the design for every page from 22 available layouts: an elegant cover, a family-photo page, and a page for her mother's favorite classroom stories. She adds old photographs from her sister, captions and dates, then invites Omar to collaborate. He accepts the invitation so he can add his own pages and pictures from abroad.

04

On the evening of the dinner, a delivery mix-up threatens the cake. Lina opens the saved bakery contact and sorts it out without hunting for Omar's messages. Omar joins the video call for the toast and reads his speech. Even while attending from another country, he adds a photo of his video-call toast and his own message to the scrapbook; Lina sees his contribution beside the family's photographs. Her mother is visibly moved that he found a way to take part.

05

After dinner, Lina and Omar finish the scrapbook together, checking the page designs, captions and photographs they each added. Lina exports the entire book as a PDF and shares it with her mother and the rest of the family so everyone can keep the celebration. She thanks Omar with a love gesture: “You were there on every page.” Their shared scrapbook remains open to both of them, even after the occasion is marked complete. Distance changed how Omar attended, not whether he mattered.

After the interview Omar opens OneSpace to mark his preparation complete and sends a brief check-in: relieved, tired, hopeful. Lina sends a proud-of-you gesture without asking for an instant result. 05

When the next appointment finally arrives, both know which documents are ready and who has the contact number. Their plan is not magic and cannot shorten a queue, but it has prevented misplaced details and unnecessary stress during an already difficult season of life.

How their day unfolded
01

On Sunday morning Lina and Omar open OneSpace over coffee at their kitchen table. Because they've chosen Living Together, Today focuses on their shared plans instead of two city clocks. 02

Omar reads out what the fridge needs while Lina adds milk, tomatoes and coffee to the shared list. They notice the washing powder is already there because Omar added it on Friday. 03

04

05

Their home isn't perfectly organized, and OneSpace doesn't clean it for them. What changes is that neither person has to keep all the reminders in their head. Every recurring list has its own new cycle, the responsibilities are visible, and they can spend the afternoon together rather than arguing about who was supposed to remember the laundry.
